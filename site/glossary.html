Reference

Transfer pricing glossary.

Plain definitions of the terms that appear in every transfer pricing file, written for people who have to use them in a document tomorrow.

In short

This glossary defines 16 transfer pricing terms: the arm’s length principle, TNMM, CUP, PLI, tested party, arm’s length range, rejection matrix, search strategy, OECD Chapter X, implicit support, DEMPE, Local File, Master File, CbCR, APA and white-label TP support.

Arm’s length principleALP
The standard that a transaction between associated enterprises should be priced as it would have been between independent parties in comparable circumstances. It is the basis of Article 9 of the OECD Model and of every domestic TP rule that follows it.
Transactional net margin methodTNMM
A method that compares the net profit margin the tested party earns on a controlled transaction with the margins earned by independent comparables. The most widely used method for services, distribution and contract manufacturing because gross-margin data is rarely comparable across companies.
Comparable uncontrolled priceCUP
A method that compares the price charged in a controlled transaction with the price in a comparable uncontrolled transaction. Preferred where the product or service is homogeneous and price data exists — commodities, royalties, interest.
Profit level indicatorPLI
The ratio used to measure profitability under TNMM — operating margin on sales, on total cost, on operating expenses (Berry ratio) or on assets. Selection must follow the functional profile of the tested party, and the reason belongs in the file.
Tested party
The entity (or the transaction of that entity) whose return is being tested. Normally the least complex party, the one that does not own non-routine intangibles and does not bear the entrepreneurial risk, because reliable comparables can be found for it.
Arm’s length range
The set of results produced by the accepted comparables. Where comparability is imperfect the range is usually narrowed to the interquartile range — the middle 50% of observations — and the tested party’s result is compared against it.
Rejection matrix
The record of every company excluded from a benchmarking search and the specific reason for each exclusion, with the evidence and source. The most frequently challenged annexure in a benchmarking file.
Search strategy
The written plan for a benchmarking search: database, geography, activity codes, independence and turnover screens, loss treatment, year set and PLI. Agreed before searching so the study is not result-driven.
OECD Chapter X
The chapter added to the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines in 2020 covering financial transactions — intra-group loans, cash pooling, guarantees, captive insurance and hedging. It requires the transaction to be delineated (is it debt?) before it is priced.
Implicit support
The benefit a group member derives from its membership of the group, without any formal guarantee. It can raise a borrower’s effective credit rating above its standalone rating and therefore lower an arm’s length interest rate.
DEMPE
Development, Enhancement, Maintenance, Protection and Exploitation — the functions that determine which entities are entitled to the return from an intangible. Legal ownership alone does not carry the return; performing and controlling DEMPE functions does.
Local File
Entity-level documentation: the local entity’s controlled transactions, functional analysis, method selection and economic analysis. Filed or maintained under domestic rules, with content largely following OECD Annex II to Chapter V.
Master File
Group-level documentation giving a high-level picture of the multinational group: structure, business drivers, intangibles, intra-group financing and financial and tax positions. Same content set across jurisdictions, following OECD Annex I to Chapter V.
Country-by-Country ReportCbCR
An annual group-level return allocating revenue, profit, tax paid and accrued, capital, earnings, employees and tangible assets across each tax jurisdiction of operation. Filed by the ultimate parent (or a surrogate) above a consolidated revenue threshold.
Advance pricing agreementAPA
An agreement between a taxpayer and one or more tax authorities fixing the TP method for future controlled transactions for a defined period, giving certainty in exchange for disclosure and process.
White-label TP support
An arrangement in which a specialist firm prepares the benchmarking or documentation and the client firm issues it under its own brand. The support firm is not named in the deliverable and has no contact with the end client.

Questions

Method questions.

What is the difference between TNMM and CUP?

TNMM compares net profit margins; CUP compares prices. CUP is more direct and therefore preferred where genuinely comparable price data exists, but it needs close product and contractual comparability. TNMM tolerates broader differences because net margins are less sensitive to product variation, which is why it dominates in services, distribution and contract manufacturing.

Which method should we use for an intra-group loan?

Interest is normally benchmarked using CUP against comparable third-party debt — bonds or loans matched on currency, tenor, rate basis, seniority and rating band — after the borrower has been credit-rated and the transaction delineated under OECD Chapter X.

Do Local File and Master File contents differ by country?

The OECD annexes give a common content set that most jurisdictions have adopted, but thresholds, filing deadlines, language and format requirements are domestic. The analysis travels; the packaging does not.
