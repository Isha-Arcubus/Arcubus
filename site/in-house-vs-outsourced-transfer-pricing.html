Comparison

In-house, Big Four, or a specialist support firm.

Three ways to get transfer pricing work done, compared on cost, control, capacity, turnaround and risk — and the situations where each is genuinely the right answer.

In short

Keep in-house what needs company knowledge and carries judgement: policy, intercompany agreements, filings, and the risk decisions. Use a Big Four or large advisory firm where you need controversy representation, cross-border opinions or a name on a contentious position. Use a specialist TP support firm for recurring, evidence-heavy production work — benchmarking searches, annual refreshes, Local File drafting, CbCR population — where cost per deliverable and turnaround matter more than brand.

Most mature tax functions use all three, and the split is decided per workstream rather than per firm.

Decision guide

Four questions that settle it.

01

Does it recur every year?

Comparables refreshes, Local File updates and CbCR population repeat on a calendar. Recurring, specified work is what outsourcing is for.

02

Does it carry a position?

If a tax authority will hold someone to it, the judgement stays inside — supported by workings from wherever they come.

03

Is the volume predictable?

Predictable volume justifies a retainer or a dedicated team. Spiky volume argues against permanent headcount.

04

Can you review what comes back?

Outsourcing needs an internal reviewer who can read a search log. Without one, you are accepting an answer you cannot test.

Questions

What tax leaders ask.

Is outsourcing transfer pricing work cheaper than doing it in-house?

Usually yes on a per-deliverable basis, because a specialist firm carries the database subscriptions and the trained analysts across many clients. But the saving only materialises if the deliverable arrives review-ready. A file your team has to rebuild costs more than doing it yourself.

What should never be outsourced?

The positions and the judgement: method selection where it is contentious, the risk appetite behind a range, disclosure decisions, and anything a tax authority will hold your name to. Those stay with your team or your adviser, informed by the workings.

How do we keep control if a support firm does the work?

Three gates: you approve the search strategy before it is run, you get preliminary results before the report is drafted, and you receive the full audit trail with the draft. If any of those is missing, you are buying an answer rather than an analysis.

Can we mix models?

Most mature tax functions do. A Big Four adviser for controversy and opinions, a specialist support firm for benchmarking volume and drafting, and the in-house team for policy, intercompany agreements and filings.

Test the model on one deliverable.

One benchmarking study is enough to judge cost, trail and turnaround. Scope and price are fixed before work starts.
