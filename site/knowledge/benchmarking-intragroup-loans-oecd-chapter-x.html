Financial transactions

Benchmarking an intra-group loan under OECD Chapter X.

A practical sequence for pricing an intra-group loan: delineate the transaction, rate the borrower, match term and currency, then build the arm’s length interest range from comparable debt.

Arcubus Advisors27 May 20268 min read

Key takeaways

  • Chapter X asks two questions in order: is the funding debt at all, and if so what is the arm’s length price? Skipping the first invites a recharacterisation argument.
  • The borrower’s standalone credit rating, adjusted for implicit group support, drives the spread more than any other input.
  • Comparable debt must match on currency, tenor, seniority, security and rate basis — a mismatch on any one of those is a bigger error than a small sample.
  • Document the options realistically available to both parties, not just the lender’s pricing.

Interest is the easiest intra-group charge to book and one of the hardest to defend. Since OECD Chapter X was added to the Guidelines in 2020, financial transactions have their own analytical sequence — and most challenges we see arise from skipping a step in it, not from a bad rate.

Step 1 — Delineate the transaction before pricing it

Chapter X asks first whether the arrangement is debt in substance. The evidence for that sits in the facts, not the loan agreement: whether there is a fixed repayment date, whether interest is actually paid, whether the borrower could service the debt out of its own cash flows, whether covenants and security exist, and whether an independent lender would have advanced the amount at all.

Record the answer explicitly. A file that opens at the pricing step tells a reviewer that the characterisation question was never asked.

Step 2 — Rate the borrower

Price follows credit risk. The sequence we use:

  • Standalone rating from the borrower’s own financials — leverage, interest cover, size, volatility and sector, run through a published rating methodology or a rating tool, with the inputs saved.
  • Implicit support adjustment — the borrower’s importance to the group, name-sharing, integration, and the group’s track record of supporting affiliates. Documented as reasoning, and capped at the group rating.
  • Instrument adjustments — seniority, security and any structural subordination move the instrument rating away from the issuer rating.

Step 3 — Match the comparables to the loan you actually have

Comparable debt — corporate bonds, syndicated or bank loans — has to match the tested loan on the dimensions that move price:

DimensionWhy it mattersCommon failure
CurrencyBase rates differ by currency; an INR loan cannot be priced off USD debt.Using a global bond set regardless of denomination
TenorThe yield curve is not flat; a 7-year loan is not a 2-year loan.Mixing tenors to enlarge the sample
Rate basisFixed and floating are not interchangeable without a swap adjustment.Comparing a fixed coupon to a floating margin
Seniority and securitySecured senior debt prices well below unsecured subordinated debt.Ignoring security in the comparable set
Issue dateRates move; pricing must reflect the market at drawdown.Using a range built at a different point in the cycle
Rating bandSpread is a function of rating.Widening the band until the tested rate falls inside

Where the sample is thin, a build-up is more defensible than a forced comparable set: risk-free rate for the currency and tenor, plus a credit spread for the rating band, plus documented adjustments for security and liquidity. Show the components.

Step 4 — Test both sides

Chapter X expects the options realistically available to both parties to be considered. For the lender that means the return available on alternative uses of the funds; for the borrower, the cost of alternative funding, including equity or a third-party facility. Two paragraphs on this closes a gap that reviewers routinely probe.

Step 5 — Write the file so the rate can be re-derived

The deliverable should let a reader reproduce the number: the delineation conclusion, the rating workings with inputs, the comparable extract with the matching table above, the range and the selected point with a reason, and the sensitivity if a key input moves. Anything asserted without a workings reference is a question waiting to be asked.

Questions

On this topic.

Can we just use the parent’s external borrowing rate?

Rarely on its own. The parent’s rate reflects the parent’s credit standing, its currency and its tenor. It can be a useful sanity check or a starting point where the loan is genuinely a pass-through, but the analysis still has to price the borrower’s own credit risk and the terms of the actual loan.

How do you handle implicit support?

Start from the borrower’s standalone rating on its own financials, then consider its strategic importance to the group and the group’s incentive to support it. The uplift, if any, is reasoned and documented — commonly a limited number of notches, capped at the group rating. The reasoning matters more than the number.

What if the borrower would not have obtained the loan at all on standalone terms?

Then the debt-capacity question is live: part of the funding may be characterised as equity, or the pricing may need to reflect terms an independent lender would have imposed, such as security or covenants. Say so in the file rather than pricing around it.

Do intra-group guarantees need separate analysis?

Yes. A guarantee is a distinct transaction with its own benefit test: the interest saving to the borrower, the risk assumed by the guarantor, and the split of that benefit. It should not be buried inside the loan rate.
