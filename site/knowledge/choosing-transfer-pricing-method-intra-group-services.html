Transfer Pricing Methods

How to choose the right transfer pricing method for intra-group services.

Key takeaways

  • The most appropriate method depends on the nature of the service, the availability of comparable data, and the degree of value creation.
  • CUP and cost-plus are the most commonly used methods for routine intra-group services; TNMM is preferred when comparables are limited.
  • Low-value-adding services can often use a simplified cost-plus approach with a 5% mark-up under the OECD Guidelines.
  • Documentation must clearly explain why the chosen method was selected and why alternatives were rejected.
  • Using the wrong method is one of the most common reasons for transfer pricing adjustments during tax audits.
Choosing the correct transfer pricing method for intra-group services is one of the most frequent sources of dispute between taxpayers and tax authorities. The OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines provide five recognised methods, but not all are equally suitable for services.

Questions

On this topic.

1. What is the most appropriate transfer pricing method for intra-group services?

There is no single method that applies to every intra-group service.The appropriate method depends on the nature of the service, the functions performed, the risks assumed, and the availability of reliable comparable data.

When can the simplified 5% mark-up approach be used?

The OECD Guidelines provide a simplified approach for certain low-value-adding intra-group services, under which a 5% mark-up may be applied to the relevant cost base, subject to the applicable conditions.The approach is intended to reduce the compliance burden where the qualifying criteria are met.
